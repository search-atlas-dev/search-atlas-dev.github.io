OTTO is the product, everything else is the shelf

Strip away the navigation and the pitch is one idea: switch OTTO on and your SEO runs itself. The site describes OTTO as an AI that handles technical fixes, on-page improvements and content, continuously building authority, site health and engagement across every site you manage, for your own brand or for agency clients. The headline claim is that it finds and fixes problems overnight so that by morning it is handled. Implementation runs through a pixel you add to your site, with a separate implementation service for teams that want it done for them and a knowledge base alongside. Everything else on the platform, and there is a great deal of it, supports or feeds that central automation. Judge the product on OTTO, because that is what you are really buying.

The module list is genuinely enormous

Counting from the navigation alone: Smart Ads, Atlas Agent, an AI Coworker, Meta Ad Studio, an Indexer, Website Studio, enterprise SEO software and LLM Visibility under featured capabilities. Local work gets citations, a Google Business Profile tool and local SEO. Content gets topical maps, Content Genius, a research tool called SCHOLAR and a report builder. Technical gets a site explorer, a site auditor and an on-page audit. Authority gets link building outreach, press release software, a backlink analyzer and QUEST. Agencies get a portfolio summary and white label options. Breadth like this is a real advantage when one subscription replaces five, and a real cost when you are paying for twenty tools to use four. Count which ones you would actually open in a month.

Beyond search results, into AI answers

One positioning choice deserves attention. The company puts getting found in AI answers on the same level as getting found on Google, naming generative and answer engine optimisation explicitly and shipping an LLM Visibility module for it. Whether anyone has a reliable method for influencing assistant answers is an open question across the whole industry, and no vendor has published a convincing one. Still, measuring whether your brand appears in those answers is tractable and worth having on a dashboard rather than in somebody's memory. Read the module as monitoring with some suggestions attached, not as a lever you can pull, and you will not be disappointed by it.

Trial terms, proof and the questions to ask

Access starts with seven days free, zero paid today and what the site describes as full access, through a registration form rather than a sales call, with pricing, demos, white label and enterprise quotes each on their own page. Proof comes as review-site badges citing more than three thousand two hundred reviews, a row of recognisable brand logos, case studies, testimonials and a set of comparison pages positioning the platform against the usual competitors. On a demo, ask how sites and keywords are counted against your plan, what happens when OTTO's automatic changes conflict with a developer's own work, and how the platform behaves if you cancel: whether on-page changes served through the pixel persist or disappear with the subscription.

Making the seven days count

  1. Bring one real site
    Connect a site you know well, not a test domain. You need to recognise whether the audit findings are genuine problems or noise, and only familiarity gives you that.
  2. Open four modules, ignore the rest
    Pick the four you believe you would use weekly and work only in those. A trial spent touring twenty tools tells you nothing about the two that would earn their keep.
  3. Review every automated change
    Log what OTTO alters before you let it run unattended. Automated on-page edits are useful when they match your intent and expensive when they quietly do not.
  4. Price it per managed site
    Divide the plan cost by the number of sites you truly manage. That number, not the feature list, decides whether an agency platform makes sense for you.

FAQ

What is OTTO in search atlas?

It is the automation layer the whole platform is built around. The site describes it as running technical fixes, on-page improvements and content by itself across every site you manage, fixing problems overnight. It installs through a pixel you add to your site, and there is a paid implementation service for teams that prefer help.

Is there a free trial?

Yes. The site advertises seven days free with nothing to pay today and full access, starting from a registration page rather than a sales call. Pricing, demos, white label details and enterprise quotes each have their own page, so check current terms there before subscribing.

Does it cover ads as well as SEO?

It lists several advertising modules, including Smart Ads and a Meta Ad Studio, alongside the SEO toolset, and the homepage describes running Google and Meta ads as part of what the platform handles. Confirm which of those sit on which plan tier before budgeting around them.

Who is it actually for?

Agencies and multi-site owners. White label, portfolio summary, client case studies and enterprise quote paths all point the same way. A single-site business can use it, but will pay for a wide module list it is unlikely to open, and simpler tools may cover the same ground.

Can it really improve visibility in AI answers?

It ships an LLM visibility module and markets generative and answer engine optimisation heavily. Treat it as measurement plus recommendations rather than a control surface, because no vendor in this category has published a reliable method for changing what an assistant says about a brand.

What does it not do for me?

It does not decide what your site should say. An audit tells you a page is thin; someone still has to write the thing worth reading, produce an image for it and publish it on a schedule. Supatraffic handles that side: SEO article writing with cover images and autopilot publishing.
